Use for donor research and relationship mapping. This skill discovers and prioritizes prospects; it does not authorize unsolicited mass outreach.

## Workflow

1. Define the current funding need, program, geography, target gift range, and acceptable donor profile.
2. Start with known relationship surfaces supplied by the organization: board, founders, volunteers, existing donors, partners, alumni, vendors, mentors, employers, and community relationships.
3. Research public evidence for each candidate's connection to the mission, geography, giving interests, institutional affiliations, and plausible relationship path.
4. Record provenance for every nontrivial claim. Do not infer wealth, protected traits, personal secrets, or private financial capacity from weak signals.
5. Rank prospects by mission fit, relationship proximity, timing, likely ability to engage, and next-best introduction path. Separate `known relationship`, `warm path`, `research-only`, and `do-not-contact`.
6. Prepare one personalized relationship brief per priority prospect: why them, why now, who can introduce, what value or update to lead with, and the smallest appropriate next step.
7. External outreach requires the configured approval policy. Never blast generic donor requests or fabricate familiarity.
8. Track responses and relationship evidence in the organization's pipeline and schedule follow-up only when appropriate.

## Output

- prioritized prospect map
- relationship-path evidence
- personalized briefing notes
- introduction requests
- follow-up dates
- do-not-contact flags

## Definition of done

The organization has a small, source-traced priority list with legitimate relationship paths and next actions, not a scraped list of wealthy names.
